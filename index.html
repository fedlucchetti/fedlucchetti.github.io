About

I am a physicist and research collaborator in the Psychosis Unit of the Centre for Psychiatric Neuroscience at Lausanne University Hospital (CHUV), where I have worked since 2023. My research uses advanced neuroimaging, in particular whole-brain magnetic resonance spectroscopic imaging (MRSI) and connectomics, to identify trait and state biomarkers of psychosis and schizophrenia, from the earliest prodromal phases onward.

My main project builds the brain's metabolic connectome: mapping how metabolite levels covary between brain regions with high-resolution 1H-MRSI, and how this organization changes across the stages of psychosis. Metabolic alterations are often invisible to structural and functional MRI, so they add a complementary layer to brain network models and could provide new biomarkers for clinical prediction. I also develop open-source software to make whole-brain MRSI analysis reproducible.

I studied physics and completed a PhD in biomedical sciences at the Université libre de Bruxelles, where my thesis combined auditory brainstem electrophysiology and computational modelling to study hearing loss. From 2020 to 2023 I was a postdoctoral researcher at the Interdisciplinary Centre for Security, Reliability and Trust (SnT), University of Luxembourg, working on resilient autonomous-driving software and federated learning.
